# P01 — Problem framing as iterative hypothesis

**Type:** RESEARCH  — **Source IDs:** DC1, DC2

## Use when
The given solution shape (a page, dashboard, form) may not fully express the underlying user problem.

## Evidence and scope
Cross treats designerly reasoning as a distinct epistemic practice; Dorst & Cross observe co-evolution in industrial design protocols. Refer to `knowledge/sources.json` for original source URLs and access limits.

## Transferable reasoning moves
1. Write the explicit user request and constraints verbatim in a short content/feature contract.
2. Form an alternative interpretation of the task as a hypothesis, not as a fact.
3. Build a small sketch or information/interaction prototype that could distinguish those interpretations.
4. Revisit the framing based on observed evidence without changing hard requirements without permission.

## Do not infer
- Do not turn problem reframing into permission to ignore specified features.
- Nine experienced industrial designers do not establish a causal effect for LLM-generated websites.

## Verify the output
Check whether the chosen concept serves the stated audience and supports the original scope; note any assumptions not tested.
