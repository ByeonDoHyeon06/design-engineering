# P10 — Allocate attention without inventing eye tracking

**Type:** RESEARCH  — **Source IDs:** R4, R5, A8

## Use when
Important content is not noticed or decorative motion competes with meaning.

## Evidence and scope
Bottom-up saliency and task-driven attention should be considered separately. Refer to `knowledge/sources.json` for original source URLs and access limits.

## Transferable reasoning moves
1. Specify what needs attention first, second and later.
2. Audit contrast, scale, placement, motion and competing focal points.
3. Use motion for a purpose: continuity, state explanation, timely feedback or meaningful storytelling.
4. Avoid attention-grabbing cues that compete with a critical action.

## Do not infer
- Saliency models cannot infer human eye-tracking results from CSS alone.
- An animation being noticeable is not the same as it being useful.

## Verify the output
Check actual rendered frames; for stronger claims run task-focused observation or appropriate eye-tracking studies.
